# Finite evidence acquisition: analysis protocol

The question is which retained evidence to retrieve next to obtain the strongest justified archive conclusion, and when permitted retrieval cannot resolve the claim. This is an isolated, model-free application of established finite reasoning and active-query methods. It neither guesses a hidden source-use label nor replays/intervenes on an agent. The [review remediation](../review_remediation/REVIEW.md) records the boundary from legacy simulator chronology.

## Design fixed before evaluation

[config.json](config.json) specifies four development problems (94100–94103, one per stratum) and 40 evaluation problems. Contiguous blocks 94200–94209, 94210–94219, 94220–94229 and 94230–94239 correspond to positive receipt acquisition, negative/completeness acquisition, multiple candidates/complementarity, and archive ambiguity. The last stratum includes globally indistinguishable use/shared-input alternatives and mixed-resolvability archives, reported separately. Strata remain separate in summaries. The generator varies archive structure, scopes, retention/dependencies, initial observations and positive costs; inputs and structural fingerprints expose repeated structures. Duplicate structures, generation errors or unfavorable outcomes are recorded, never replaced on performance grounds.

Each problem has at most 64 worlds, eight queries and two outcomes per query. World priors are uniform over declared valid generative assignments, a design assumption rather than an empirical incident distribution. Costs are integers in {1,2,4} drawn during generation. A cost is an abstract retrieval unit, not dollars, bytes or human effort. Initial evidence and authenticity/completeness assumptions remain explicit even when no paid query is necessary. Model validation independently enforces event chronology, completeness scope and event-versus-retention distinctions. No input history comes from the stochastic simulator.

## Policies and terminal conditions

All five policies receive the same initial observations, possible-world model and public query catalogue. They use the same certificate checker and full-signature preprocessing. Actual realized answers reach planners only after a charged lookup. No query reveals a source-selection label. Policies are read-all, schema-aware fixed order, world-entropy per cost, claim-directed pair-cut per cost, and exact optimal adaptive planning. [METHOD.md](METHOD.md) gives the fixed objectives and tie rules. These are acquisition comparisons, not comparisons of a sound checker with a guessing baseline.

A nonempty compatible-world set entails established or ruled-out only when all claim values agree. Archive irreducibility requires every still-possible full-answer signature cell to contain opposite claim values. A single ambiguous cell is insufficient if another continuation resolves the claim. Inconsistent histories produce an explicit error state, never vacuous entailment. Unresolved pending and budget exhaustion are distinct from proved archive irreducibility.

Read-all is the exhaustive operational reference: it retrieves the catalogue before its normal terminal check. Under a budget cutoff, an already available certificate is honored, including at budget zero. The other policies stop on a verified terminal certificate before selecting another query. Budgets are floor(0%,25%,50%,75%,100% × total catalogue cost). If the next selected query exceeds the remaining budget, stop; no budget-specific reranking or query substitution is permitted. A currently available certificate takes precedence over a budget-exhausted classification.

## Frozen measurements

For every problem, policy and budget, enumerate every full-answer signature and weight it by its exact prior mass. Signatures, worlds, queries and policy evaluations are not independent experimental units. Save per-problem metrics and stratified means; no sampling intervals are calculated for complete finite-model expectations. Report archive-resolution probability (established or ruled out), archive-irreducibility probability, unresolved and budget-exhausted outcomes, inconsistent outcomes, incorrect/unverifiable certificates and false irreducibility separately.

Measure expected and worst-case retrieval cost, expected/worst query count and canonical answer bytes, preprocessing/planning runtime, exact-solver state counts, and minimum-cost hindsight retrieval-result certificates. Hindsight examines all subsets of the full observed query history, under the same initial evidence and fixed interface. It is a lower bound, not an executable policy or the smallest physical evidence set. Report paired pair-cut differences against schema-aware and entropy policies and gaps to exact optimal expected cost. Ratios use only problems with positive optimal cost; list zero-optimum problems separately, including globally irreducible archives. Mechanical soundness is a shared-checker consequence, not a scientific discovery.

## Freeze and execution chronology

Development uses only the four development seeds. Focused invariants and independent tiny-tree optimality tests must pass before freezing. The execution freeze pins generated inputs, selection/generation log, structural fingerprints, all local policy/model/checker/generator/evaluator code, configuration, and this protocol/method before any evaluation policy runs. It records development results and dependency isolation. The evaluator verifies those pins and refuses to overwrite results. Evaluation failures and flat or reversed comparisons are retained without changing cases, models, objective functions or tie rules.

An optional descriptive wiki check may use at most four deterministically selected distinct histories from the already local pinned export, after the finite study is complete. It is restricted to fixed-rule inserted-reference classification, not source use, finite-model optimality or real-world impossibility. It must not rerun the census, download data, or introduce an importer. Raw text stays local. The technical result records whether this check actually ran and why.
