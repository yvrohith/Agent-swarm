# Prior robustness and structural sensitivity: analysis protocol

This is a review-informed, post-baseline sensitivity analysis of the completed [finite evidence-acquisition study](../evidence_acquisition/ANALYSIS.md). Its question is whether measured acquisition-cost differences survive changes in the supplied deployment prior and the weighting of repeated problem structures, and how much retrieval cost a wrong planning prior adds without changing the evidentiary possibilities. The original outcomes are already known. This is neither an independent confirmation nor a preregistered discovery.

## Fixed inputs and objective

Reuse all 40 saved evaluation problems and the four separate saved development problems. The evaluation seeds are 94200–94239, with ten problems in each of `positive_receipt`, `negative_completeness`, `complementary_candidates`, and `archive_ambiguity`. Development uses 94100–94103. Do not regenerate inputs or select replacements. Keep possible worlds, claims, initial evidence, authentic-record semantics, completeness assumptions, query catalogues, query costs, and terminal rules fixed. This study does not import or rerun the legacy stochastic simulator.

The sole acquisition objective is full-budget expected weighted retrieval cost to the existing terminal archive class. Full budget means the sum of catalogue query costs; it introduces no budget-dependent reranking. Read-all retains its exhaustive operational behavior, while the other policies retain their certificate stopping rules. The policies are the unchanged `read_all`, `schema_aware`, `world_entropy`, `pair_cut`, and `exact_optimal`. Their algorithms, floating-point entropy calculation, rational cost calculations, and cost/opaque-ID tie rules remain as implemented in the frozen source. No new heuristic, cost grid, world-size grid, or budget-resolution objective is introduced.

Before new comparisons, reaggregate the original saved full-budget rows, including stratum counts, paired cost differences, zero-optimum problems, and fingerprints. Check saved signature histories against their recorded costs, query counts, returned bytes, and certificate status. A material discrepancy blocks affected comparisons; it cannot be repaired by silently replacing historical results or changing this design. Preserve the original positive-receipt loss, all other unfavorable historical results, and any discrepancy record.

## Two weighting rules

Report both the original equal-problem mean over 40 inputs and a structure-balanced mean. For the latter, first average members of each existing unweighted structural fingerprint group, then average groups equally. Apply the same rule separately within each stratum. Preserve every original problem row and report group membership and size.

The saved fingerprints have been recomputed directly from the saved problems. There are 30 groups: ten pairs and twenty singletons, with 6, 8, 8, and 8 groups respectively in the four strata listed above. The fingerprint retains claim, structural choices, initial evidence, normalized query semantics, and the assignment/claim/query-answer table; it removes query IDs, catalogue order, and query costs. [METHOD.md](METHOD.md) gives the exact construction and the additional equality check on repeated groups. Members can have different prices, opaque-ID tie breaks, or catalogue order and therefore different policy trajectories. These are configuration variants, not independent incidents or a proved general graph-isomorphism class. Grouping is fixed before new policy comparisons and will not be adjusted after observing rankings.

## Five priors and two planning modes

[config.json](config.json) fixes exactly five strictly positive rational distributions over the original support: the original assignment prior `q0`, signature-balanced `qS`, full-archive-class-balanced `qT`, sparse retained-record tilt `qMinus`, and dense retained-record tilt `qPlus`. Their formulas are in [METHOD.md](METHOD.md). Retained-record counting uses the existing typed schema, includes actual completeness records, counts each nonempty catalogue action once, and excludes empty-lookup wrappers even when those wrappers have bytes. Duplicate actions each count once as specified; this does not make their observations independent.

Keep coincident distributions and label their coincidence. Do not replace them with stronger perturbations, prune low-probability worlds, or use a probability threshold for certainty. These priors are declared model workloads, not estimates of incident frequencies. Class balancing provides a hypothetical distribution to a planner, never the actual archive class or realized signature.

The primary analysis, `frozen_p0`, retains each original policy's behavior under the original prior. Reweight saved per-signature histories by the complete deployment mass of their signature cells; do not pass the new prior into the planner. The secondary analysis, `matched_q`, replans entropy, pair-cut, and exact adaptive under each supplied deployment prior with unchanged algorithms. Read-all and schema-aware reuse their prior-independent paths. The exact planner in this second mode is the matched-prior optimum, a reference with the supplied distribution rather than a method that learns it from logs.

The design has 40 problems × 5 priors × 2 modes × 5 policies, or 2,000 policy cells before failures. These are repeated analyses of the same finite problems, not 2,000 independent observations. Retain both modes and all prior labels even when paths or distributions coincide. A heuristic can beat the frozen original-prior exact planner under another prior without contradicting optimality. It cannot beat a valid matched-prior optimum under the same information, costs, and terminal objective.

## Saved quantities and summaries

For every problem, prior, mode, and policy, save exact rational and readable decimal expected retrieval cost, query count, and canonical returned bytes. Keep established, ruled-out, and archive-irreducible probability masses separate. Their masses may change when the same fixed archive classes receive different weights. Save signature masses, coincident-prior labels, original structural membership, certificate verification status, and failed or capped computations.

Report pair-cut minus schema-aware and pair-cut minus world-entropy costs, with lower/tied/higher problem counts. Report each policy's excess cost and ratio relative to the matched-prior optimum, and the frozen exact planner's deployment penalty. Keep zero-optimum problems explicit and exclude undefined ratios with their denominator stated. A ratio of aggregate means must not be described as the mean of per-problem ratios. Provide global and per-stratum summaries under both weighting rules; do not hide a stratum reversal behind the global mean.

No sampling intervals, bootstrap, p-values, or significance claims are appropriate for these complete finite-model expectations. Returned bytes, query count, abstract retrieval units, CPU time, wall time, and money are distinct quantities. No conversion between them is assumed.

## Correctness gates and freeze

Development checks use only the four existing development inputs and existing tiny test fixtures. They check exact normalization and signature-mass aggregation; unchanged support, initial evidence, query outcomes, costs, signature cells, and archive classes; prior-independent certificate status for fixed histories; agreement between saved-cost reweighting and direct development calculation; original-prior reproduction; structure-balanced weighting; and absence of realized-world or actual-signature input to planners. Every produced certificate must pass the existing independent checker. Certificate/model hashes can change when the declared prior changes even though compatible-world sets and logical status do not.

An independent tiny-tree enumerator checks exact optimality on nonuniform-prior, complementary-query, correlated-answer, constant-query, and irreducible-archive fixtures. It enumerates decision trees, rather than calling the same dynamic program through another wrapper. Verify the elementary positive-prior invariance and alpha/beta comparison in [METHOD.md](METHOD.md), including zero-optimum identities. Check the matched-prior optimum against all feasible reported policies. These are mechanical validation consequences, not new scientific discoveries.

After these development checks pass, freeze configuration, analysis code, imported scientific dependencies, saved input references, probability transformations, grouping, and this protocol/method before new evaluation comparisons. The computational dependency check must not depend on presentation files or unrelated historical documentation. Do not overwrite a frozen run. A post-freeze code defect requires a versioned amendment and preservation of failed outputs. A capped or failed exact solve is unavailable, never an optimum; retain the problem and display missing denominators. The fixed exact-solver cap is 100,000 memoized states per problem/prior solve, with no performance-based replacement or tuning.

## Cold-cache planning workload

Use only the original prior on all 40 evaluation problems, with exactly three recorded repetitions and the fixed policy order in [config.json](config.json). Validate one shared immutable model per problem. For every policy/problem/repetition create a fresh planner with empty policy caches and construct its complete reachable history tree from the initial information state. At each selected query, traverse every compatible outcome in lexicographic outcome-ID order. This is the same construction procedure for every policy; it does not select a realized world. Read-all exhausts its catalogue, while the other policies stop at their unchanged terminal rule. Cache reuse is allowed only within that single construction.

Measure process CPU and wall time separately for shared model validation/signature preprocessing, planner initialization and decisions, leaf certificate construction, independent certificate checking, traversal bookkeeping/model-immutability checks, and canonical tree/certificate serialization. Record decision/choice/history/leaf counts, exact-solver states, tree hashes, and all three repetitions, including failures or caps. Certificate counts can differ because different policies create different trees. Report this precisely as local cold-cache policy construction, not per-investigation latency or a scalability study. Do not select a favorable repetition or compare these measurements to the historical cache-sharing workload as if the scopes were identical.

## Scope and stopping rule

Complete the fixed study even if weighting removes an average advantage, priors reverse rankings, or exact planning is preferable on these sizes. Do not retune priors or pair-cut to recover a preferred result. Interpret the results only within supplied finite supports, authentic evidence, valid completeness declarations, costs, and terminal rules. Positive-prior robustness does not test forged evidence, omitted mechanisms, unreliable declarations, or unknown real deployment distributions.

Preserve all historical scientific artifacts and accounting. This task permits no model calls, network requests, credential access, package downloads, additional spend, new wiki analysis, presentation artifacts, or remote writes. Finish with exact offline reproduction instructions, validation and preservation results, failed/unfinished denominators, and the observed decisions in the new technical results report.
